# Legacy Site Analysis — mindyrobinson.wordpress.com

Scope of analysis:
- https://mindyrobinson.wordpress.com/
- https://mindyrobinson.wordpress.com/biography/

This document summarizes what exists on the legacy WordPress.com site today, what it communicates well, what’s missing for conversion (bookings/press/collabs), and what to carry forward into a modern landing page.

---

## 1) Executive summary

The legacy site reads like a **single long “About” post** with mixed goals: biography + credits + image gallery + comments. It contains valuable credibility signals (credits, IMDb link, booking email, mailing address), but the presentation is **dense, dated, and not conversion-optimized**.

Most importantly for a prospect:
- The booking contact (email) is present, but **not elevated** into a clear, persistent CTA.
- The page includes **unmoderated comments**, including hostile/abusive content, which can undermine brand trust.
- The biography page provides a personal narrative, but it lacks scannable structure (high-level highlights, credits, press, media kit, and clear contact paths).

## 4) UX + conversion analysis (from a prospect’s viewpoint)

### 4.1 What a producer/brand typically wants quickly
1. Clear positioning (actress/model/host + niche)
2. Visuals (hero image + curated gallery)
3. Reel/trailers (embedded)
4. Key credits + press highlights (scannable)
5. Contact (booking email/agent/manager)
6. Downloadable EPK/press kit

### 4.2 Current gaps on the legacy site
- **No clear “book/inquire” call-to-action above the fold** (email is present but buried)
- **Text density** (long paragraphs, low scannability)
- **No modern reel embed** on the homepage experience
- **No structured press section** (logos/links/quotes)
- **No clear separation of audiences** (casting vs press vs brand collab)
- **Public comments** introduce reputation risk
- **WordPress.com chrome** (subscribe/login bars) distracts from the core goal

### 4.3 What works well today
- Strong credibility: high volume of appearances, recognizable titles
- Clear direct booking email is present
- IMDb link is present
- Visual content exists

## 5) Content strategy recommendations (what to carry forward)

### 5.1 Keep (high-value legacy content)
- The main positioning line, tightened:
  - “Character actress with 175+ TV & film appearances.”
- The top 5–8 most impressive credits (curated)
- Booking email and official social links
- IMDb link
- A small “press kit / EPK available upon request” callout

### 5.2 Reframe (same content, better structure)
- Replace long paragraphs with:
  - A short “one-liner”
  - 3 feature bullets (Acting / Modeling / Collaborations)
  - A “selected credits” list
  - A “reel” section

### 5.3 Remove or isolate
- Public comments and trackbacks (or move to a moderated platform)
- WordPress subscription UI elements (not possible on WP.com, but solvable in a custom site)

### 5.4 Tone considerations
The biography page includes explicit language in quotes. If the site’s goal is professional inquiries, consider:
- Keeping the authenticity but offering a **brand-safe version** of the bio for producers/brands
- Optionally having a longer “personal story” page for fans, separated from the booking funnel

## 6) Information architecture proposal (modern replacement)

A conversion-oriented replacement can be:
- **One-page landing** + optional subpages.

Recommended one-page sections:
1. Hero (name, roles, strongest social proof, primary CTA)
2. Work highlights (reel embed + selected credits)
3. Press (logos/links/quotes)
4. Gallery (curated stills)
5. Contact (booking/press/collab with separate pathways)
6. Footer (links + legal)

Optional pages:
- Biography (rewritten into scannable sections)
- Press kit / EPK (download or request)
